What Kind of Instrument Is a Kazoo?

2026.09.05
VerifiedKAZOO WONDERLAND editorial status

The short answer

A kazoo is an instrument that sets a thin membrane resonating with your voice and changes the color of that voice. What decides the pitch is not the body of the instrument, but the voice of the person playing it. Its mechanism is different from a whistle that you simply blow air into.

FROM THE 2016 ARCHIVE

In October 2016, KAZOOMUSIC.NET published a page titled “What is a KAZOO?” At the time it introduced the kazoo as a membrane instrument (a mirliton), and explained that “its prototype came from Africa, and the kazoo in its present form was born in America.”

Re-examining the sources over these ten years, we found that the first half of that explanation — “an instrument that vibrates a membrane with the voice” — still holds, while the origin story needs to be written much more carefully.

Re-examined in 2026

On a kazoo, the sound the player sings, speaks, or hums vibrates a thin membrane, which adds distinctive overtones and color to the original voice. It is an instrument better described by “putting your voice into it” than by “blowing.”

Early sources include US patents relating to voice-and-membrane instruments and to the kazoo's construction: Carlo Arpisella in 1877, Simon Seller in 1879, Warren Herbert Frost in 1883, and George D. Smith in 1902. Frost's 1883 patent in particular names the instrument “kazoo” — but because voice-and-membrane structures existed before it, we do not simply declare 1883 “the year the kazoo was invented.”

What we know about the origin today

The famous story of Alabama Vest, Thaddeus von Clegg, Macon in the 1840s, and the 1852 Georgia State Fair has been told in many kazoo-related sources. However, in KAZOO WONDERLAND's 2026 research we have not yet confirmed any contemporary primary sources — newspapers, fair records, patents, or product advertisements — that support that story.

For that reason we do not erase those tales; we keep them as tradition, presented separately from the patent history that can be confirmed.

Why rewrite it ten years on

KAZOO WONDERLAND is not a site that pretends its old articles never existed. We keep what we knew in 2016 and how we presented it, and we make visible what changed when we researched it again in 2026. Step by step, we are updating the history of this small instrument into something more certain.
